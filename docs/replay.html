Fortis Advisors v. Johnson & Johnson (Auris Health)

milestones, not a metric

Refused — not expressible in the signed terms

  • the trigger is an event (FDA regulatory clearance, one of several milestones whose total count the sources report inconsistently), not a metric value. The contract compares a number to a target; an event needs a party who signs that the event happened, and the model has no template for that — MetricAttestation carries a figure, not a fact.

PUBLIC RECORD, and the figures below are the ones two or more sources agree on. J&J/Ethicon's acquisition of Auris Health: up to $2.35 billion in contingent consideration tied to FDA regulatory milestones for the iPlatform and Monarch devices (Arnold & Porter advisory, Mar 2026), none of which was achieved after a change in the FDA's required clearance pathway. One named milestone is a $100 million 'Soft Tissue Ablation Milestone' (same source); an earlier summary describes it as a 510(k) clearance for lung tissue ablation. CAUTION: the litigation history is reported inconsistently across secondary sources — a post-trial Chancery award of more than $1 billion, a stipulated judgment of roughly $811 million in January 2026, and a Delaware Supreme Court decision of January 12, 2026 (No. 490,2024) that reversed the first-milestone ruling and vacated the related damages. This file does not rely on any of those figures; the only facts it uses are the milestone trigger and that no earnout was paid.

In re SwervePay Acquisition

'up to $53.75 million' against a payment-volume baseline that was itself contested

Refused — not expressible in the signed terms

  • payout type "sliding" — the tranche is all-or-nothing. Tiered, sliding, interpolated and formula payouts — the common shapes in the public record — are different computations and would have to be signed as such.
  • 2 metrics — one metric per agreement. Two metrics means two agreements, or a metric defined as a formula before it reaches the ledger.

PUBLIC RECORD. In re SwervePay Acquisition, LLC (Del. Ch., July 31, 2026). Per Holland & Knight's summary of the opinion: sellers received 'approximately $9.9 million in cash and an additional $10 million in rollover equity at closing', with contingent consideration of 'up to $53.75 million tied to the Target's ability to convert the Platform's existing payments volume onto its own systems during the 2021 earnout period', hinging on 'a single, contested metric: the volume of payments actually flowing through the Platform's systems' — represented as $34 billion, actually about $5.2 billion. Award: $75,692,297 on fraud. Other summaries split the contingent consideration into two milestones ($43.75m and $25m) and larger equity figures; the discrepancy is left unresolved here rather than picked, because it does not change the outcome below.

Winshall v. Viacom (Harmonix)

3.5x the excess over a rising threshold, uncapped

Refused — not expressible in the signed terms

  • payout type "formula" — the tranche is all-or-nothing. Tiered, sliding, interpolated and formula payouts — the common shapes in the public record — are different computations and would have to be signed as such.
  • the target changes by period (32000000, 45000000) — the signed terms carry one target for every period, so a rising or stepped target is a different contract.

PUBLIC RECORD. Winshall v. Viacom International Inc., Del. Ch. (2011), aff'd Del. (2013). The opinion states the selling stockholders 'became entitled to receive cash payments equal to three and a half times the excess, if any, over Gross Profit thresholds of $32 million in 2007 and $45 million in 2008.' The opinion does not disclose Harmonix's actual Gross Profit for either year or the amounts ultimately paid, so no figures are asserted here.

Synthetic: Cumulative Ratchet

Synthetic — a cumulative target with a sliding payout, which this ledger cannot express

Refused — not expressible in the signed terms

  • periodBasis "cumulative" — the contract evaluates each period against a fixed target; a cumulative or catch-up basis would need the signed terms to carry running state, which they deliberately do not.
  • payout type "sliding" — the tranche is all-or-nothing. Tiered, sliding, interpolated and formula payouts — the common shapes in the public record — are different computations and would have to be signed as such.
  • caps/floors on the tranche — not expressible; the tranche amount is a single signed figure.
  • schedule has 2 periods but the terms declare 8. The contract requires every period reported, in order, exactly once.

SYNTHETIC. Written deliberately outside the signed-terms model. If a practitioner's real clause looks like this, the harness must say so instead of quietly approximating it.
